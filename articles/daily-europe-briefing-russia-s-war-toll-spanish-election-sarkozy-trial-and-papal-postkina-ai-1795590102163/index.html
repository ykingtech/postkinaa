Daily Europe Briefing: Russia's War Toll, Spanish Election, Sarkozy Trial, and Papal Revelations

By PostKina Editorial · 25 November 2026 at 08:01 Europe/Paris · 5 min read

Russia's war in Ukraine reveals a staggering human cost, while Spain faces a pivotal election. Former French President Sarkozy and his wife head to trial, and late Pope Francis' diary entries spark spiritual debate.

What happened

Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine has fundamentally altered mortality patterns within Russia, with leaked data indicating nearly half a million deaths. This revelation coincides with a BBC journalist's investigation into her own family's deaths in Ukraine, highlighting the war's complex human toll. Meanwhile, Spain faces a pivotal general election this Sunday, marked by deep political divisions and the potential for a far-right party to enter government. In France, former President Nicolas Sarkozy and his wife, Carla Bruni, are set to face trial, adding to Sarkozy's history of legal challenges. Separately, published extracts from the late Pope Francis' diary claim he experienced three visions of the devil, offering a rare glimpse into his spiritual life.

The revelation of nearly 500,000 deaths within Russia due to the Ukraine war is profoundly significant, offering an internally sourced glimpse into the true human cost, which official figures have consistently downplayed. This vast number could have long-term demographic and societal repercussions for Russia. The personal investigation into family deaths in Ukraine underscores the profound human tragedy, illustrating how lives are shattered and the challenges in discerning truth amidst wartime propaganda. Spain's upcoming election is crucial, potentially bringing a far-right party into government, reflecting broader European political polarization. For Nicolas Sarkozy, the impending trial reinforces legal accountability for high-ranking political figures, demonstrating the robustness of the French justice system. Finally, claims from Pope Francis' diary about encountering the devil offer unique insight into a prominent religious leader's spiritual struggles, potentially sparking theological discussions and influencing perceptions of the papacy.

Should a far-right party gain influence, it could alter Spain's stance on key EU policies like migration and climate change, contributing to a growing right-wing bloc within the EU. Pope Francis' diary revelations resonate across Europe's Christian nations, particularly among Catholics, contributing to cultural and religious discourse and influencing perceptions of the Church's leadership.

Background and context

The Ukraine conflict escalated with Russia's full-scale invasion in February 2022, following years of tensions. The war has caused widespread destruction and loss of life, with official casualty figures often disputed. The leaked Russian mortality data offers a rare internal perspective, contrasting with the Kremlin's opaque reporting. The personal account of family deaths highlights information warfare and challenges civilians face in attributing blame. Spain's political landscape is increasingly fragmented. The upcoming election follows a period of coalition governments, reflecting deep divisions. A far-right party entering government would mark a significant shift from Spain's post-Franco political consensus. Nicolas Sarkozy, French President from 2007-2012, has faced several legal challenges post-presidency, including a 2021 corruption conviction. This latest trial adds to his high-profile legal proceedings. Pope Francis, pontiff until his death, was known for progressive views. His posthumously published diaries offer an intimate look into his spiritual life.
