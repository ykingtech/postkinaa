U.S. Envoys Arrive in Moscow Ahead of Ukraine Talks with President Putin

By PostKina Editorial · 12 November 2026 at 13:02 Europe/Paris · 3 min read

Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff landed in Moscow on Thursday, preparing for a high‑stakes meeting with Vladimir Putin that could shape the future of the Ukraine conflict. The U.S. delegation will soon head to Dublin to join President Volodymyr Zelensky in a separate summit.

How we report: This article adds context and explanation to attributed source reporting. AI may assist the editorial workflow; sources, corrections and licensing remain visible to readers.

What happened

On Thursday, 12 November 2026, former U.S. President Donald Trump’s son Jared Kushner and former U.S. Treasury Secretary Steve Witkoff arrived in Moscow. Their arrival marked the beginning of a diplomatic mission aimed at negotiating with Russian President Vladimir Putin over the ongoing war in Ukraine. The delegation is scheduled to travel to Dublin later that day to meet with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky.

Key facts

  • Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff are the U.S. envoys in Moscow.
  • The meeting with Putin is intended to discuss the Ukraine conflict.
  • The delegation will depart for Dublin on the same day to meet Zelensky.
  • The trip follows a series of U.S. diplomatic efforts to de‑escalate tensions.
  • The mission is part of a broader U.S. strategy to influence Russian policy.

Why it matters

The presence of high‑profile U.S. figures in Moscow signals a renewed attempt by Washington to engage directly with Russia on the Ukraine war. If successful, the talks could lead to a ceasefire or a new framework for peace, potentially ending years of conflict that have displaced millions and strained global security. The U.S. delegation’s approach also reflects a shift from sanctions‑heavy tactics to diplomatic outreach, a strategy that could reshape international relations in the post‑war era.

European impact

Europe’s security architecture is deeply intertwined with the Ukraine conflict. A breakthrough in Moscow could reduce the risk of spill‑over into neighboring countries and ease the burden on European energy supplies, which have been disrupted by Russian actions. Moreover, a negotiated settlement would likely influence the European Union’s stance on defense spending and its relationship with NATO, potentially altering the balance of power on the continent.

Background and context

The Ukraine war began in 2014 with Russia’s annexation of Crimea and escalated into a full‑scale invasion in February 2022. Since then, the United States has imposed extensive sanctions on Russia and supplied Ukraine with military aid. Despite these measures, the conflict has persisted, causing widespread humanitarian crises and destabilizing the region.

Jared Kushner, a former senior adviser to President Trump, has been involved in U.S. foreign policy discussions since 2017. Steve Witkoff, who served as Treasury Secretary under President Obama, has a long history of diplomatic engagement. Their selection reflects Washington’s desire to combine political influence with economic expertise.

What happens next

The U.S. delegation is expected to hold a formal meeting with President Putin in Moscow on 13 November. Outcomes will be closely monitored by European governments and the international community. Following the Moscow talks, the delegation will convene with President Zelensky in Dublin to discuss any agreements reached and to coordinate further diplomatic efforts. The next phase will involve assessing the feasibility of implementing any negotiated terms and determining the role of the United States in supporting a post‑war reconstruction of Ukraine.
