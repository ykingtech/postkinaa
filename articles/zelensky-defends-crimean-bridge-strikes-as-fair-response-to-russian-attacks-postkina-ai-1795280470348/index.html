Zelensky Defends Crimean Bridge Strikes as 'Fair Response' to Russian Attacks

By PostKina Editorial · 21 November 2026 at 18:01 Europe/Paris · 4 min read

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky has characterized recent strikes on the Crimean Bridge as a "fair response" to Russia's extensive aerial campaign targeting Ukrainian bridges. His comments came during a meeting with UK politician Wes Streeting, highlighting Kyiv's retaliatory stance.

How we report: This article adds context and explanation to attributed source reporting. AI may assist the editorial workflow; sources, corrections and licensing remain visible to readers.

What happened

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky has publicly stated that Kyiv's actions against the Crimean Bridge constitute a "fair response" to Russia's sustained aerial assaults on Ukrainian infrastructure. The President's remarks were made during a meeting with Wes Streeting, a prominent figure in UK politics. Zelensky specifically cited 109 distinct Russian aerial attacks on bridges across Ukraine as the justification for the strikes targeting the strategically vital Crimean Bridge, which connects mainland Russia to the annexed Crimean Peninsula. This assertion frames the Ukrainian operations as a direct and proportional retaliation within the ongoing conflict.

Key facts

  • Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky described strikes on the Crimean Bridge as a "fair response."
  • Zelensky attributed these actions to 109 separate Russian aerial attacks on Ukrainian bridges.
  • The statement was made during a meeting with UK politician Wes Streeting.
  • The Crimean Bridge is a critical logistical and symbolic link between Russia and occupied Crimea.

Why it matters

President Zelensky's explicit justification for the Crimean Bridge strikes marks a significant public declaration of Ukraine's strategic intent and rationale. It underscores a shift towards a more overt retaliatory posture, directly linking Ukrainian actions to prior Russian aggression against civilian and military infrastructure. The Crimean Bridge, a symbol of Russia's annexation of Crimea and a crucial supply route for its forces, holds immense strategic and psychological value. Attacks on it, and Kyiv's subsequent claims of responsibility, signal Ukraine's capacity and willingness to target high-value assets within Russian-held territory, potentially escalating the conflict and challenging Russia's perceived control over annexed regions. This public stance also aims to rally international support by framing Ukrainian actions as defensive and proportionate.

European impact

The ongoing targeting of critical infrastructure, including bridges, by both sides in the conflict has profound implications for European stability and security. The Crimean Bridge, as a key logistical artery, impacts Russia's ability to sustain its military operations, which in turn affects the broader geopolitical landscape of Eastern Europe. Any escalation involving such high-profile targets raises concerns about the potential for wider regional instability, disrupting trade routes, energy supplies, and humanitarian efforts. European nations, many of whom are significant supporters of Ukraine, closely monitor these developments for their potential to prolong the conflict, increase refugee flows, or necessitate further economic and military aid. The destruction or damage of vital links also complicates post-conflict reconstruction and regional connectivity, posing long-term challenges for the continent.

Background and context

The Crimean Bridge, also known as the Kerch Bridge, is a dual-purpose road and rail bridge spanning the Kerch Strait, connecting the Taman Peninsula of Russia with the Kerch Peninsula of Crimea. Its construction began after Russia's annexation of Crimea in 2014 and it was opened in 2018 (road) and 2019 (rail). The bridge serves as a vital logistical artery for supplying Russian military forces in Crimea and southern Ukraine, as well as for civilian traffic. For Russia, it is a powerful symbol of its territorial claim over Crimea and its engineering prowess. For Ukraine, it represents an illegal occupation and a legitimate military target due to its role in sustaining the invading forces. The conflict has seen both sides target critical infrastructure, with Russia frequently employing missile and drone strikes against Ukrainian energy facilities, transportation networks, and civilian areas, while Ukraine has increasingly demonstrated a capability to strike targets deep within Russian-controlled territory.

What happens next

The explicit justification by President Zelensky for the Crimean Bridge strikes signals a continued readiness by Ukraine to engage in retaliatory actions against Russian military and logistical targets. This stance is likely to be met with further condemnation from Moscow and could potentially lead to intensified Russian attacks on Ukrainian infrastructure in response. Diplomatically, Ukraine will likely continue to seek international understanding and support for its actions, framing them as necessary defensive measures. The ongoing targeting of critical infrastructure by both sides suggests a protracted conflict where strategic assets remain key objectives. The international community will closely monitor any further escalations, particularly concerning the targeting of high-value infrastructure, and assess the implications for regional stability and the prospects for a peaceful resolution.
