Daily Europe Briefing: Europe Briefing: Starlink, Media Freedom, and Oligarch Dreams

By PostKina Editorial · 16 November 2026 at 08:02 Europe/Paris · 4 min read

Developments across Europe include Ukrainian calls for Starlink access, Czech protests over public media funding, and Paris police restrictions on demonstrations. Meanwhile, Russian tycoons reportedly envision a post-Putin era, and football legend Ronaldinho makes a comeback.

How we report: This article adds context and explanation to attributed source reporting. AI may assist the editorial workflow; sources, corrections and licensing remain visible to readers.

What happened

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky has appealed to Elon Musk and the United States to lift restrictions on the Starlink satellite internet service, citing its importance in countering Russian attacks. In the Czech Republic, thousands of people demonstrated against a government proposal to alter the funding of public media, expressing concerns about potential impacts on media freedom. Paris authorities have implemented a ban on protests near specific educational institutions for several days. Separately, reports suggest that some wealthy individuals in Russia are anticipating a future political and economic landscape that could resemble the 1990s, a period marked by significant influence of business magnates.

Key facts

  • Ukrainian President Zelensky requested the lifting of Starlink restrictions.
  • The Ukrainian president stated that internet disruptions hinder efforts to stop Russian attacks.
  • Protests occurred in the Czech Republic against proposed changes to public media funding.
  • Paris police have banned demonstrations near 10 schools and five universities.
  • The ban in Paris is in effect from November 16 to November 20.
  • Reports indicate some Russian tycoons are considering a post-Putin future.
  • Brazilian football legend Ronaldinho has returned to play at age 46.

Why it matters

President Zelensky's call for unrestricted Starlink access highlights the critical role of advanced communication technology in modern warfare and national defence. The situation underscores the ongoing struggle for information superiority in the conflict with Russia. The protests in the Czech Republic signal a deep-seated concern among citizens regarding the independence of public service media, a cornerstone of democratic societies. Any perceived erosion of media freedom can have far-reaching consequences for public discourse and accountability. The police action in Paris, while framed as a security measure, raises questions about the balance between public order and the right to protest, particularly in proximity to educational establishments. The reported aspirations of Russian business figures suggest a potential undercurrent of political dissent or strategic planning among influential elites, hinting at possible future shifts in Russia's internal dynamics.

European impact

The Ukrainian request for Starlink access has implications for the broader European security architecture, as disruptions to communication networks can affect regional stability. The Czech Republic's debate over public media funding resonates across the European Union, where similar discussions about the sustainability and independence of public broadcasters are common. The Paris protest ban, while localised, reflects a broader European trend of increased security measures in urban centres, impacting civil liberties. The reported sentiments among Russian tycoons could, if they translate into action, influence geopolitical relationships and economic ties involving European nations.

Background and context

Starlink, a satellite internet constellation operated by SpaceX, has been instrumental in providing internet connectivity in Ukraine since the full-scale Russian invasion began. However, its use has faced scrutiny, with reports of restrictions impacting military operations. Public service media in Europe, funded through various models including licence fees and state budgets, are often seen as vital for providing independent journalism. Changes to their funding mechanisms can be contentious. Paris, like many major European capitals, frequently sees demonstrations, and authorities often implement security measures to manage public order. The political and economic landscape in Russia has been shaped by the influence of oligarchs since the dissolution of the Soviet Union, with their power fluctuating depending on the Kremlin's policies.
