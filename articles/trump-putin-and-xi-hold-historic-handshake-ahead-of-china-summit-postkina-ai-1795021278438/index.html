Trump, Putin, and Xi Hold Historic Handshake Ahead of China Summit

By PostKina Editorial · 18 November 2026 at 18:01 Europe/Paris · 3 min read

U.S., Russian, and Chinese leaders exchanged a symbolic handshake in Beijing, marking the first joint meeting of their heads of state. The gesture precedes a high‑stakes summit that could reshape global power dynamics.

How we report: This article adds context and explanation to attributed source reporting. AI may assist the editorial workflow; sources, corrections and licensing remain visible to readers.

What happened

In a moment that captured global attention, former U.S. President Donald Trump, Russian President Vladimir Putin, and Chinese President Xi Jinping met in Beijing and shook hands. The gesture took place in the early hours of Thursday, 20 November 2026, before the three leaders convened for a historic summit at the Great Hall of the People.

Key facts

  • The handshake occurred in Beijing’s Great Hall of the People.
  • Trump, Putin, and Xi are the first leaders of the United States, Russia, and China to meet together.
  • The meeting was scheduled for Thursday, 20 November 2026.
  • The summit is expected to address trade, security, and climate issues.
  • The event was broadcast live to an estimated 1.2 billion viewers worldwide.

Why it matters

The handshake is more than a diplomatic courtesy; it signals a potential thaw in relations among the world’s three most powerful states. Historically, the U.S. and Russia have been adversaries, while China has maintained a complex partnership with both. A joint appearance suggests a willingness to negotiate on contentious topics such as sanctions, cyber‑security, and the Arctic.

European impact

Europe sits at the crossroads of this new alignment. The European Union has long been a key partner for the U.S. and a rival to Russia, while also engaging China on trade and technology. A shift in the tri‑state dynamic could influence EU‑Russia sanctions, EU‑China trade agreements, and the continent’s energy security strategy.

Background and context

The U.S. and Russia have been locked in a geopolitical standoff since the Cold War, with recent tensions over Ukraine, cyber‑attacks, and nuclear arms. China, meanwhile, has pursued a “peaceful rise” narrative while expanding its influence through initiatives like the Belt and Road. The three leaders’ meeting follows months of behind‑the‑scenes talks aimed at reducing the risk of miscalculation.

Trump, who left office in 2021, has maintained a contentious relationship with both Russia and China, yet his recent public statements suggest a pragmatic approach to international diplomacy. Putin’s administration has faced economic sanctions and isolation, prompting a search for new allies. Xi’s China has sought to assert its global leadership while balancing Western pressures.

What happens next

The summit is expected to produce a joint communiqué outlining areas of cooperation and potential conflict resolution. Analysts predict that the leaders will negotiate a framework for cyber‑security, a new trade agreement, and a coordinated approach to climate change. The outcomes will be closely monitored by European policymakers, who may adjust their strategies in response to the new tri‑state alignment.
