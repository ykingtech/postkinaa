Alleged 'King of Germany' Faces Trial for Leading Criminal Group

By PostKina Editorial · 6 November 2026 at 13:01 Europe/Paris · 3 min read

Peter Fitzek, who claims to be the 'king' of a self-proclaimed 'Kingdom of Germany,' is on trial accused of heading a criminal organization. The group sought to establish its own state.

How we report: This article adds context and explanation to attributed source reporting. AI may assist the editorial workflow; sources, corrections and licensing remain visible to readers.

What happened

Peter Fitzek, who styles himself as the 'king' of a self-declared entity known as the 'Kingdom of Germany' (KRD), is facing trial. He is accused of heading a criminal organization. The KRD is described as a banned group that aimed to establish its own sovereign state, separate from the Federal Republic of Germany.

Key facts

  • Peter Fitzek is on trial accused of leading a criminal group.
  • The group is known as the 'Kingdom of Germany' (KRD).
  • The KRD sought to establish its own state.
  • Fitzek is associated with the Reichsbürger movement.
  • The KRD is described as a banned group.

Why it matters

The trial of Peter Fitzek highlights the persistent challenge posed by groups like the Reichsbürger to the established order in Germany. These movements, which often deny the legitimacy of the modern German state, can attract individuals who engage in criminal activities or pose security risks. The KRD's ambition to create its own kingdom underscores the ideological underpinnings of such groups and their rejection of democratic institutions. The legal proceedings aim to address the alleged criminal activities associated with Fitzek's leadership.

European impact

While the direct impact of the KRD's activities may be primarily domestic to Germany, the phenomenon of extremist groups challenging state authority resonates across Europe. Similar movements exist in other European countries, often drawing on nationalist, anti-immigrant, or conspiracy-driven ideologies. The success or failure of German authorities in prosecuting individuals like Fitzek could influence how other European nations approach similar internal security threats. The spread of disinformation and the erosion of trust in government institutions are transnational concerns that these cases bring to the fore.

Background and context

Peter Fitzek is associated with the Reichsbürger (Citizens of the Reich) movement. This broad network of individuals and groups in Germany denies the legitimacy of the Federal Republic of Germany, often believing that the German Reich in its pre-World War I or pre-World War II borders continues to exist. Members may refuse to pay taxes, possess illegal weapons, or engage in other activities that challenge state authority. Some Reichsbürger adherents have been involved in violent incidents. The 'Kingdom of Germany' appears to be one manifestation of this broader ideological current, seeking to create a physical or administrative structure that mirrors a sovereign state, complete with its own leadership and purported territory.
