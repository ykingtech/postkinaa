Student‑Backed MPs Demand Resignations After Serbia’s Political Shake‑Up

By PostKina Editorial · 30 October 2026 at 13:01 Europe/Paris · 3 min read

Following the ousting of Aleksandar Vučić’s ruling party, Serbia’s Student List representatives have called for the resignation of key political figures, sparking a debate over accountability and democratic norms.

How we report: This article adds context and explanation to attributed source reporting. AI may assist the editorial workflow; sources, corrections and licensing remain visible to readers.

What happened

In the wake of a dramatic shift in Serbia’s political landscape, the Student List, a coalition of student‑led parties that recently won seats in the National Assembly, has publicly demanded that several senior politicians step down. The call follows the removal of President Aleksandar Vučić’s Serbian Progressive Party (SNS) from power after a series of protests and a contentious parliamentary vote that saw the opposition coalition gain a majority.

Key facts

  • The Student List secured 12 seats in the 2026 parliamentary election, a record for a student‑led coalition.
  • SNS was ousted from power after a 60‑vote majority in the National Assembly on 28 October 2026.
  • Student List MPs have called for the resignation of former Prime Minister Ana Brnabić and other senior SNS officials.
  • The demand was issued via a joint statement released on 29 October 2026.
  • The statement cites alleged corruption, abuse of power, and failure to uphold democratic norms.
  • No formal resignation has yet been submitted by the targeted officials.
  • The opposition coalition has pledged to form a new government if the resignations occur.
  • The Student List’s call has sparked debate across Serbian media and civil society.

Why it matters

The Student List’s demand represents a rare instance of a youth‑driven political group directly challenging entrenched political elites. Their insistence on accountability signals a broader shift in Serbian politics, where younger voters are increasingly demanding transparency and reform. If the resignations materialise, it could accelerate the transition to a new governing coalition and potentially reshape Serbia’s policy agenda, particularly on issues such as media freedom, judicial independence, and EU integration.

European impact

Serbia’s political realignment has implications for the European Union’s enlargement strategy and the stability of the Western Balkans. The EU has long monitored Serbia’s progress toward accession, and a clear break from the long‑standing SNS dominance could be viewed positively by Brussels. Moreover, the Student List’s activism may inspire similar movements across the region, reinforcing democratic norms and potentially easing tensions in neighboring countries.

Background and context

The Serbian Progressive Party, led by Aleksandar Vučić since 2012, has dominated the country’s political scene for over a decade. Critics have long accused the party of consolidating power, curtailing press freedom, and engaging in corrupt practices. The 2026 election saw a surge in support for opposition groups, including the Student List, which capitalised on widespread dissatisfaction among young voters.

The Student List, formed in 2024, is a coalition of student‑led parties from universities across Serbia. Their platform focuses on education reform, anti‑corruption measures, and increased civic engagement. Their electoral success marked a significant moment for youth participation in Serbian politics.

What happens next

The Student List’s demands have set the stage for a potential political crisis. If the targeted officials refuse to resign, the opposition coalition may seek to form a minority government, relying on support from other parties. Alternatively, a negotiated settlement could involve a reshuffle of key positions or the appointment of independent oversight bodies. The outcome will likely influence Serbia’s trajectory toward EU membership and its internal democratic consolidation.
