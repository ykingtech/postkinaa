Australia and Philippines Forge New Defence Pact Amid South China Sea Tensions

By PostKina Editorial · 15 November 2026 at 18:01 Europe/Paris · 5 min read

Australia and the Philippines have formalized a new defence agreement, deepening military cooperation as Manila navigates persistent tensions with China in the South China Sea. The pact is part of a broader strategy by the Philippines to bolster regional alliances.

How we report: This article adds context and explanation to attributed source reporting. AI may assist the editorial workflow; sources, corrections and licensing remain visible to readers.

What happened

The Philippines and Australia have signed a new defence pact, significantly deepening their bilateral military relationship. This agreement emerges as Manila actively strengthens its security posture and alliances amidst ongoing maritime disputes in the South China Sea. The pact is set to include joint exercises, the provision of surveillance equipment, and enhanced coordination between their armed forces. This development is part of a broader strategic realignment by the Philippines, which is also fostering closer military ties with Japan and the United States. This concerted effort aims to diversify security partnerships and enhance capabilities through collaborative defence initiatives in the contested waters.

Why it matters

This new defence pact between Australia and the Philippines holds considerable geopolitical significance for the Indo-Pacific region. It signals a deepening commitment from two democratic nations to regional security and stability, particularly in an area marked by complex territorial disputes. For the Philippines, the agreement provides crucial support and capability enhancement as it navigates persistent challenges to its sovereignty and maritime rights in the South China Sea. By formalizing and expanding military cooperation with Australia, Manila gains access to advanced training, technology, and strategic partnership, bolstering its defence readiness and maritime domain awareness.

From Australia's perspective, the pact reinforces its strategic interests in maintaining a free and open Indo-Pacific. Canberra's engagement with Manila contributes to a network of alliances designed to uphold international law and norms. The agreement also serves as a clear message about the importance of collective security in addressing regional challenges. The broader trend of the Philippines strengthening ties with Australia, Japan, and the United States indicates a concerted effort to create a more robust security architecture. This collective approach aims to deter aggression, promote stability, and ensure unimpeded commerce through vital international waterways, influencing broader power dynamics across the Indo-Pacific.

European impact

While geographically distant, the defence pact between Australia and the Philippines carries global implications relevant to European interests. European economies rely heavily on global trade routes, many traversing the South China Sea. Any escalation of tensions or disruption to maritime commerce in this vital waterway could significantly impact European supply chains, energy security, and market stability. Therefore, regional efforts to maintain peace and uphold international maritime law indirectly benefit European prosperity.

Furthermore, European foreign policy emphasizes a rules-based international order. Challenges to sovereignty and freedom of navigation in the South China Sea test this order. By supporting nations like the Philippines in defending their rights and engaging in multilateral security cooperation, European countries indirectly reinforce their own diplomatic principles. The deepening of alliances in the Indo-Pacific can also influence global power balances, potentially affecting European strategic considerations and diplomatic engagements worldwide. While not a direct military alliance with Europe, the pact contributes to a broader geopolitical landscape that European policymakers must monitor for its potential impact on global security and economic stability.

Background and context

The South China Sea is one of the world's most contested maritime regions, with multiple nations, including the Philippines, China, Vietnam, and others, asserting overlapping territorial claims. These claims encompass vast areas of ocean and numerous islands, believed to hold significant resources and through which substantial global trade passes. The Philippines has been particularly vocal in asserting its sovereign rights against perceived encroachments, especially around features within its exclusive economic zone (EEZ).

Tensions have escalated in recent years, marked by incidents involving vessels and patrols. China's expansive claims, often delineated by its "nine-dash line," were largely rejected by an international arbitral tribunal in 2016, a ruling Beijing has refused to acknowledge. In response to these persistent challenges, the Philippines has strategically sought to bolster its defence capabilities and forge stronger alliances with like-minded nations. This approach aims to enhance its maritime security, improve its ability to monitor and respond to incidents, and strengthen its diplomatic leverage through collective security arrangements. The new pact with Australia, alongside partnerships with Japan and the United States, represents a cornerstone of this strategy.

What happens next

The signing of this defence pact is expected to usher in intensified military cooperation between Australia and the Philippines. Observers can anticipate an increase in joint military exercises, enhancing interoperability and strengthening capabilities. The implementation of provisions related to surveillance equipment suggests a focus on improving maritime domain awareness, crucial for monitoring activities in the South China Sea. Closer coordination will likely translate into more integrated planning and response mechanisms for regional security challenges.

This agreement is also viewed within the broader context of the Philippines' evolving security strategy. As Manila continues to deepen military ties with Japan and the United States, the Australian pact will contribute to a more interconnected network of alliances, aiming for a more resilient security framework in the Indo-Pacific. The reaction from other regional actors, particularly China, will be closely watched. The long-term impact will depend on practical cooperation, the evolving geopolitical landscape, and the continued commitment of all parties to upholding international law and promoting regional stability.
