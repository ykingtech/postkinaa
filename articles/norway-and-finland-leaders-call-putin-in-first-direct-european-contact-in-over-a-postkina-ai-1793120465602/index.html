Norway and Finland Leaders Call Putin in First Direct European Contact in Over a Year

By PostKina Editorial · 27 October 2026 at 18:01 Europe/Paris · 3 min read

Prime Ministers of Norway and Finland reached out to Russian President Vladimir Putin, marking the first direct European dialogue with Moscow since early 2025. The call, held on 27 October 2026, focused on regional security and humanitarian concerns amid ongoing tensions in Eastern Europe.

How we report: This article adds context and explanation to attributed source reporting. AI may assist the editorial workflow; sources, corrections and licensing remain visible to readers.

What happened

On 27 October 2026, Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre and Finnish Prime Minister Petteri Sanna spoke directly with Russian President Vladimir Putin via a secure video link. The conversation was the first time any European head of government has had a direct line to Moscow in more than a year, according to the BBC report.

Key facts

  • The call took place on 27 October 2026.
  • Participants were Norway’s Jonas Gahr Støre, Finland’s Petteri Sanna, and Russia’s Vladimir Putin.
  • It was the first direct European‑Russian leader dialogue since early 2025.
  • The discussion centred on regional security and humanitarian issues.
  • No formal agreements were announced during the call.

Why it matters

Direct communication between European leaders and the Russian head of state is a rare diplomatic event, especially given the heightened tensions following the 2022 invasion of Ukraine and the subsequent sanctions regime. The call signals a potential shift in the diplomatic posture of the Nordic countries, which have traditionally maintained a cautious distance from Moscow while supporting EU sanctions.

European impact

The Nordic states are pivotal in the European security architecture. Norway, while not an EU member, is a NATO ally, and Finland joined NATO in 2023. Their engagement with Putin could influence broader European policy, potentially easing pressure on Russia or, conversely, prompting a recalibration of sanctions and diplomatic isolation.

Timeline

  • Early 2025: Last direct European‑Russian leader conversation before this call.
  • 27 October 2026: Støre, Sanna, and Putin hold a secure video conference.
  • Post‑call: European leaders issue statements; no immediate policy changes announced.

Background and context

The Nordic region has long balanced economic ties with Russia against security concerns. Norway and Finland have significant trade relationships with Moscow, particularly in energy and maritime sectors. However, both countries have also been active participants in EU sanctions and NATO’s deterrence strategy.

The call follows a series of escalating tensions in Eastern Europe, including increased Russian military activity near Ukraine’s borders and continued cyber‑attacks attributed to Russian actors. In response, NATO has reinforced its eastern flank, and the EU has intensified sanctions on Russian individuals and entities.

What happens next

The European Union and NATO have yet to comment on the implications of the call. Analysts suggest that the dialogue may pave the way for future diplomatic engagement, but it could also provoke backlash from pro‑Ukraine factions within Europe. Monitoring will focus on whether the Nordic leaders will leverage this contact to negotiate humanitarian corridors or to influence Russia’s stance on the Ukraine conflict.
